Titel (60/100 tekens):
Why does your whole town share one clock? Blame the railways

Beschrijving:
For most of history, every town kept its own time by the sun. Then trains arrived, and one timetable met dozens of different clocks.

What the sources say:
- Local mean time varied across Britain: Plymouth ran about 16.5 minutes behind Greenwich, Liverpool 12 minutes.
- 1847: the Railway Clearing House recommended that all railway stations adopt Greenwich time.
- 1852: the railways distributed Greenwich time to stations by telegraph.
- 1880: Greenwich Mean Time became legal time in Great Britain.
- 18 November 1883, the "Day of Two Noons": US railroad station clocks were reset to standard time as noon arrived in each zone.
- 1918: the Standard Time Act, the first US federal law on standard time.
