Why does your whole town share one clock? Blame the railways.

For most of history, every town kept its own time by the sun. In Britain, Plymouth ran about 16.5 minutes behind Greenwich, Liverpool 12 minutes. Fine, until one train timetable had to work across all of them.

In 1847 the Railway Clearing House recommended Greenwich time for every station. By 1852 the railways sent it down the telegraph, and in 1880 it became legal time in Great Britain. In the US, railroad clocks were reset on 18 November 1883: the "Day of Two Noons".

Sources: Royal Museums Greenwich, "Why do we have Greenwich Mean Time?"; Library of Congress, "The Day of Two Noons".
