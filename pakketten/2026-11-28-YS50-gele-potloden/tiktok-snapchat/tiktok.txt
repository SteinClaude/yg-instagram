Why are so many pencils yellow?

It goes back to the 1889 World's Fair in Paris, where a pencil maker showed off a yellow pencil. The best graphite was believed to come from China, and in China yellow stood for royalty and respect. The pencil was even named after a famous diamond: the Koh-i-Noor.

Soon other pencil makers painted their pencils yellow too.

And the twist: pencils never had lead in them. The core is graphite mixed with clay.

So your yellow pencil is dressed like royalty, and it has no lead.
