Non-stick pans started with a gas cylinder that seemed empty.

In 1938, chemist Roy Plunkett stored a gas called TFE in small cylinders at a DuPont lab. When one was opened, nothing came out, yet it weighed the same as before.

Inside was a white powder: the gas had turned into a polymer. It resisted heat and chemicals, and almost nothing would stick to it. Its first use was in the Manhattan Project; pots and pans came years later. We know it as Teflon.

Source: Science History Institute, "Roy J. Plunkett".
