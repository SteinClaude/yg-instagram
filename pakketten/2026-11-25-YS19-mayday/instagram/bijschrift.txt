Why do pilots say "Mayday"? It's French (sort of).

Mayday is the spoken distress call on the radio. It comes from the French "m'aider", help me, written the English way.

In February 1923, The Times reported that the distress signal "S.O.S." would give way to "May-day". The call is credited to Stanley Mockford, a radio officer at Croydon Airport in London. In 1927, an international radio convention in Washington made MAYDAY the official spoken distress call.

Sources: ITU, International Radiotelegraph Convention (Washington, 1927); Historic Croydon Airport Trust; Wordorigins.org, "mayday".
