Why are golf balls full of dents?

In the early days of golf in Scotland, the balls were smooth. Then golfers discovered that their old, beat-up balls went farther than the new, smooth ones. So they started denting their brand-new balls on purpose. By 1905, golf balls were made with dimples.

Why it works: the dents make the air cling to the ball a little longer, so the wake behind it gets smaller and pulls it back less. A dimpled ball can fly nearly twice as far as a smooth one.

Sources: Penn State News (2007); The Physics FAQ.
