Why are train tracks exactly 4 feet 8½ inches apart?

More than half of the world's railway routes use this odd number: 1,435 mm. Early horse-and-cart trackways were already around 4 ft 8 in wide, and George Stephenson used the gauge on old coal tramways serving mines near Newcastle.

By the time he designed his railways, it was a well-known measure. The Liverpool & Manchester, the world's first intercity railway, opened in 1830. In 1846, a British law made 4 ft 8½ in the rule for new passenger railways.

Sources: Douglas Puffert, "Path Dependence" (EH.net); Network Rail, "George Stephenson"; Railway Regulation (Gauge) Act 1846.
