Why is Bluetooth named after a Viking king?

Harald "Bluetooth" Gormsson was a Danish king more than a thousand years ago. His nickname is said to come from a dead, blue-grey tooth. His runestone claims he won all of Denmark and Norway.

In the late 1990s, engineer Jim Kardach needed a code name for a radio link that would unite computers and phones. "Bluetooth" was only a placeholder, but the other names didn't work out in time. The logo? Harald's initials, in runes.

Sources: Bluetooth SIG, "Origin of the name"; National Museum of Denmark, "The Jelling Stone"; Jim Kardach (kardach.com).
