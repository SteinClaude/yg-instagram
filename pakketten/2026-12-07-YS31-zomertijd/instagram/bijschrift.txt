Why do we change the clocks? It started in World War I.

Benjamin Franklin only joked about it: his 1784 essay was satire. In 1895, New Zealand insect collector George Hudson proposed moving the clocks two hours every spring, and was ridiculed. In 1907, William Willett paid for a pamphlet, "The Waste of Daylight".

Then came the war. On 30 April 1916, Germany and Austria-Hungary became the first countries to introduce daylight saving time, to save coal. Britain passed its law that May.

Sources: The Franklin Institute; New Zealand Department of Internal Affairs (2023); Western Front Association (2020).
