## Dictionary.com, lemma's "lb", "pound" en "libra"
https://www.dictionary.com/browse/libra

- lb.: "Origin of lb. < Latin lībra" (https://www.dictionary.com/browse/lb)
- pound: "ultimately from Latin pondō "by weight, in weight" (adverb), in the phrase libra pondō "a pound by weight"" (https://www.dictionary.com/browse/pound)
- libra: "the ancient Roman pound (containing 5053 grains or 327.4 grams)"; als sterrenbeeld "literally, pair of scales"

## Arika Okrent, "Why is 'Pound' Abbreviated as 'lb'?", Mental Floss (30 aug 2014)
https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/52058/

- "Lb is an abbreviation of the Latin word libra."
- "The primary meaning of libra was balance or scales (as in the astrological sign),"
- "We got the word "pound" in English from the pondo part of the libra pondo" … "but our abbreviation comes from the libra."

## Royal Mint Museum, "Pounds, shillings and pence"
https://www.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/journal/history/pounds-shillings-and-pence/

- "The £ sign developed over the years from the letter L, the initial letter of the Latin word libra meaning a pound of money."

## Wat bewust niet gezegd is
- Britannica (plan-bron) gaf op 7 nov een 403. Niet gezegd: wanneer het streepje door "lb" (℔) of £ kwam (onbekend), en een exact gewicht anders dan "about 327 grams" (schattingen lopen tot ±329 g). De £ is al YS15; hier alleen één zin als verwijzing. Weegschaal en letters zijn een eigen tekening.
