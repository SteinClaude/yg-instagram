Fold A4 in half and it keeps its shape. Here's why

Beschrijving:
A4 looks like a random size: 210 by 297 mm. It is not. Its sides follow the square root of two, so every half has the same shape.

What the source says:
- In the ISO paper sizes, height divided by width is always the square root of two (1.4142 : 1).
- Cut such a page in half and the result has the same width/height ratio again.
- A0 has an area of exactly one square metre; A4 is 210 × 297 mm.
- One of the oldest written records of the √2 ratio for paper is a letter by Georg Christoph Lichtenberg to Johann Beckmann, dated 25 October 1786.
- A French law on paper taxation of 3 November 1798 already defined formats that correspond exactly to the modern ISO sizes.
- Germany adopted the sizes as standard DIN 476 in 1922, reinvented by Dr. Walter Porstmann.
- In 1975 it became an international standard (ISO 216) and the official United Nations document format. Today it is used almost everywhere, except North America.

Source:
Markus Kuhn, "International standard paper sizes" (University of Cambridge): https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~mgk25/iso-paper.html
