1. Why does an hour have sixty minutes? Not ten. Not a hundred.
2. The number comes from ancient Mesopotamia. The Babylonians counted in base sixty, from the Sumerians.
3. Why sixty? Nobody knows for sure.
4. But sixty splits nicely: by 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20 and 30.
5. The hours came from Egypt. Twelve parts for the light, twelve for the dark. A twenty-four hour day.
6. Around the year 150, the astronomer Ptolemy split each degree into sixty parts. And each of those into sixty again.
7. The first small part, in Latin: partes minutae primae. The minute.
8. The second small part: partes minutae secundae. The second.
9. But clocks didn't count minutes and seconds until many centuries later.
10. Your clock still counts the Babylonian way.
