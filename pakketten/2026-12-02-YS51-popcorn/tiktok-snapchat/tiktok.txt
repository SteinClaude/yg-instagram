Why does popcorn pop?

Every kernel is a tiny pressure cooker: a small drop of water inside a hard shell. Heat turns that water into steam, and the pressure builds until the shell bursts. At 180 °C almost every kernel pops; at 170 °C only about a third do.

The soft starch puffs up and turns the kernel inside out. Scientists think the "pop" sound comes from the steam escaping, not from the shell cracking. And the jump? A little leg of starch pushes off the pan like a spring.

All of it happens in a few hundredths of a second.

Sources: Virot & Ponomarenko, Journal of the Royal Society Interface (2015); Scientific American; Purdue University; USDA National Agricultural Library; Popcorn Board.
