Why does a week have seven days?

Most likely, thank the ancient Babylonians. They saw seven special lights in the sky: the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn. Seven became a holy number, and it fit the Moon too: a month of about 29 days splits into four parts of about seven.

The Romans named the days after those seven lights. Sunday is the Sun's day, Monday the Moon's day, Saturday Saturn's day. They also kept an older eight-day cycle, until Emperor Constantine banned it in 321.

In 1929 the Soviet Union switched to a five-day week, with a different day off for every worker. Families and friends often couldn't rest on the same day. In 1931 they tried six days, and in 1940 they went back to seven.

Sources: Atlas Obscura (2015); The Conversation (2024); The Old Farmer's Almanac (2026).
