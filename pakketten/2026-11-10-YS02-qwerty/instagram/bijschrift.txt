Why isn't your keyboard in alphabetical order?

The first typewriter keyboards were. Christopher Latham Sholes, a Milwaukee printer, patented an early typewriter with colleagues in 1868, with the keys in A-to-Z order. Then he kept changing the layout for years.

The famous answer, "to stop the keys jamming", is contested: E and R, a very common pair, sit right next to each other. Two researchers at Kyoto University argued in 2011 that early users shaped it: telegraph operators transcribing Morse code.

QWERTY won when the big typewriter makers merged in 1893 and made it the standard.

Source: Smithsonian Magazine, "Fact of Fiction? The Legend of the QWERTY Keyboard".
