Why isn't your keyboard in alphabetical order?

Beschrijving:
The first typewriter keyboards were alphabetical. So how did we end up with QWERTY? The famous answer (to stop the keys jamming) is contested, and nobody fully agrees on the real one.

What the source says:
- Christopher Latham Sholes, a Milwaukee printer, and colleagues patented an early typewriter in 1868. Its keys were in alphabetical order.
- Sholes kept changing the layout over the following years.
- The "jamming" story is contentious: E and R, a very common pair, sit right next to each other.
- Koichi and Motoko Yasuoka (Kyoto University, 2011) argue the layout was shaped by early users: telegraph operators transcribing Morse code.
- Remington started selling the typewriter in 1874 for $125. In 1893 the big makers merged into the Union Typewriter Company, which made QWERTY the de facto standard.

Source:
