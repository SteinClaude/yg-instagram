Why do onions make you cry?

Cutting an onion breaks open its tiny cells and lets out an enzyme called alliinase. That starts a chain reaction: a second enzyme makes a gas that drifts up into the air and reaches the nerves in your eyes. Your eyes answer with tears.

Why would an onion do that? To protect itself. It's chemical warfare against animals that want to eat it.

Tip: chill the onion for about 30 minutes before you cut it, and leave the root end on.

So next time an onion makes you cry, it's just self-defense.

Sources: Smithsonian Magazine; National Capital Poison Center (Poison Control).
