Alexander Graham Bell wanted you to answer the phone with "Ahoy".

The inventor of the telephone recommended "Ahoy, ahoy". On 15 August 1877, Thomas Edison suggested something else in a letter: "Hello". Early telephones had no bell, and Edison wrote that a "Hello!" could be heard 10 to 20 feet away.

Edison's word won. It wasn't new, though: "hello" was already being written in America by 1826.

Sources: Hagley Museum & Library; Wordorigins.org, "hello"; NPR, "Lost and Found Sound" (1999).
