Why is a stop sign an octagon? (It used to be yellow)

Beschrijving:
Why does a stop sign have eight sides? In the early 1920s, US highway engineers ranked danger by shape, so drivers could react to a sign even when they couldn't read it at night. And for decades, stop signs weren't red at all.

What the source says:
- Early 1920s: engineers from Wisconsin, Minnesota and Indiana classified sign shapes by the level of danger. Round = railroad crossing (most danger), octagon = stop, diamond = caution, rectangle = directions and information.
- The reasoning: there was little illumination on signs, so drivers should respond to the shape even when they couldn't see the letters.
- A 1924 report favoured yellow for stop signs; red was rejected because of poor visibility at night.
- 1954: stop signs became white letters on red, mainly thanks to new fade-resistant finishes.

Source:
U.S. Federal Highway Administration, "The Evolution of MUTCD" (archived copy): https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20161031205947/http:/mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/kno-history.htm
