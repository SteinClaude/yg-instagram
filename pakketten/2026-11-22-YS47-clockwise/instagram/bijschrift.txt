Why do clocks go clockwise?

Before clocks, people told time with sundials. In the northern half of the world, the sun crosses the southern sky, so a sundial's shadow sweeps around in the direction we now call clockwise. When clocks came along, their hands copied it.

South of the equator, the sun is in the northern sky and the shadow turns the other way. If clocks had been invented down there, "clockwise" would mean the opposite.

So who picked clockwise? The sun did.

Sources: The Irish Times (2001); The Old Farmer's Almanac.
