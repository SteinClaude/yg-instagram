1. The pound sign is an L. In disguise.
2. L for libra, the Latin word for a pound of money.
3. Over the years, that L became the pound sign.
4. Old British money was written as pounds, S, and D.
5. S for solidus, D for denarius. Both originally Roman.
6. One pound was twenty shillings, or two hundred and forty pence.
7. The system grew up in the Frankish kingdom, and reached England perhaps in the seventh century.
8. Under William the First, silver pennies got a consistent weight. That is where the pound of two hundred and forty pence comes from.
9. Britain counted like this until 1971.
10. Every pound sign you see: an L, for libra.
