Why is the pound sign an L?

The £ sign grew out of the letter L, for the Latin word libra: a pound of money. Old British money was written £ s d, pounds, shillings and pence, and the s and d are Latin too: solidus and denarius, originally Roman.

A pound was 20 shillings, or 240 pence. Britain counted its money that way until 1971.

Source: Royal Mint Museum, "Pounds, shillings and pence".
