Titel (48/100 tekens):
Why do we say OK? It started as a newspaper joke

Beschrijving:
OK began in 1839 as a joke abbreviation in a Boston newspaper. A presidential campaign the next year kept it alive.

What the sources say:
- 1839: Boston newspapers were full of humorous abbreviations, like O.F.M. ("Our First Men"), S.P. ("Small Potatoes") and R.T.B.S. ("Remains To Be Seen").
- March 23, 1839: in the Boston Morning Post, editor Charles Gordon Greene used "O.K." for "oll korrect", a deliberate misspelling of "all correct".
- Linguist Allen Walker Read of Columbia University found this earliest use by reading old newspapers.
- 1840: supporters of President Martin Van Buren, born in Kinderhook, New York, called him "Old Kinderhook", shortened to "OK" on rally signs; an O.K. Club formed in New York City.
- Van Buren lost the election, but "OK" stayed.
