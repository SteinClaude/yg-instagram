Why are flamingos pink?

They aren't born pink. Flamingo chicks hatch with white-gray feathers. The pink comes later, from their food: algae and tiny brine shrimp, full of red and orange pigments called carotenoids. Their body processes those pigments, and the feathers turn pink.

They even eat with their heads upside down, using their bill as a filter. In zoos, a pigment is added to their food to keep them pink. And parents feeding their chicks can fade to pale pink, or even white.

Flamingos really are what they eat.

Sources: Smithsonian's National Zoo; San Diego Zoo.
