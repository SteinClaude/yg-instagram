Why is the right side of a ship called "starboard"?

Not because of the stars. Before ships had a rudder in the middle, they were steered with a big oar. Most sailors were right-handed, so the oar went on the right side: the steer-board. In Old English, "steer" plus "board" (the side of a boat) became starboard.

The other side was the loading side at the dock, called larboard. Larboard and starboard were far too easy to mix up, so in 1844 the Royal Navy ordered "port" instead: the side that faces the dock.

Sources: NOAA National Ocean Service; word histories (2016).
