What does SOS stand for? Nothing.

Beschrijving:
SOS is not an abbreviation. It is one continuous Morse pattern, chosen because it is simple and unmistakable.

What the source says:
- The SOS signal originated in German national radio regulations in 1905.
- It was adopted internationally at the International Radiotelegraph Conference in Berlin in 1906, where 29 countries signed a convention: ships in distress had to use this signal.
- It is a continuous group of Morse dots and dashes (three dots, three dashes, three dots), not separate letters. It was chosen because the pattern is simple and unmistakable.
- Because it can be read as "SOS" in Morse code, that became the usual way to remember it.
- Before that, Marconi operators used "CQD": "CQ" was a general call to all stations, "D" stood for distress.
- On 15 April 1912, the Titanic sent CQD and, as suggested by assistant operator Harold Bride, mixed in the new signal, SOS.
- SOS stopped being the official distress signal on 1 January 1998, replaced by satellite systems and voice messages.
