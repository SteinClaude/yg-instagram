The barcode started as dots and dashes drawn in sand.

On a beach in Miami, Joe Woodland was thinking about dots and dashes. He poked four fingers into the sand, pulled his hand toward him, and had four lines: wide and narrow, instead of dots and dashes.

His first barcode was round, like a bullseye. With his former classmate Bernard Silver he applied for a patent in 1949. The rectangular code we use today was developed later at IBM. In 1974, a pack of chewing gum in Troy, Ohio, became the first retail item ever scanned.

Source: WYSO (NPR member station), "50 years ago, the first retail barcode was scanned at an Ohio grocery store" (2024).
