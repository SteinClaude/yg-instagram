Velcro started with a walk with a dog.

In 1941, George de Mestral came home from a walk in the woods with burrs stuck to his pants and to his dog's fur. Under a microscope, a burr turned out to be covered in thousands of tiny hooks that gripped the loops in his clothes.

He made the hooks from nylon, working with a fabric maker in Lyon, France, and patented the hook-and-loop fastener. The name mixes two French words: velour (velvet) and crochet (hooks).

Source: National Inventors Hall of Fame, "A Walk in the Woods: The Story of VELCRO".
