Where does the dollar sign come from? Even the US Bureau of Engraving and Printing says its origin "has been variously accounted for". The most accepted explanation: "Ps", short for the Spanish peso.

Why is the emergency number 999? It started with a fire.

In November 1935, a fire on Wimpole Street in London killed five women. The review that followed led to a committee on how telephone operators could recognise emergency calls.

The world's first traffic light exploded.

In December 1868, London got the first traffic lights, outside the Houses of Parliament. Railway signalling engineer J P Knight took the idea from the railways: semaphore arms by day, red and green gas lamps at night, all worked by a police constable.

Then a leak in the gas line made one of the lights explode, seriously burning the policeman on duty. The trial was scrapped, and traffic lights disappeared from the UK for more than 40 years.

Why is the pound sign an L?

The £ sign grew out of the letter L, for the Latin word libra: a pound of money. Old British money was written £ s d, pounds, shillings and pence, and the s and d are Latin too: solidus and denarius, originally Roman.

Why does longitude 0° run through Greenwich?

In 1884, the Greenwich Meridian was recommended as the Prime Meridian of the World. The main reason: most of the world was already using it.

The USA had already based its own time zones on Greenwich, and in the late 19th century 72% of the world's commerce relied on sea charts that used Greenwich as 0°. Choosing it helped the largest number of people. The exact line is marked by the cross-hairs of a telescope: the Airy Transit Circle.

Why are train tracks exactly 4 feet 8½ inches apart?

More than half of the world's railway routes use this odd number: 1,435 mm. Early horse-and-cart trackways were already around 4 ft 8 in wide, and George Stephenson used the gauge on old coal tramways serving mines near Newcastle.

By the time he designed his railways, it was a well-known measure. The Liverpool & Manchester, the world's first intercity railway, opened in 1830. In 1846, a British law made 4 ft 8½ in the rule for new passenger railways.

Why does an hour have 60 minutes? Not 10, not 100.

Sixty is ancient. The Babylonians calculated in base 60, a system they inherited from the Sumerians. Why the Sumerians chose it isn't clear, but 60 divides evenly by 1 to 6, and by 10, 12, 15, 20 and 30.

The Egyptians split light and dark into 12 hours each: 24 in all. Around AD 150, the astronomer Ptolemy divided each degree into 60 parts, and each of those into 60 again. The first small parts became the "minute", the second ones the "second".

Sources: Michael A. Lombardi (NIST), Scientific American (2007); BBC News (2026).

New every day at 18:00 (Amsterdam).

Why does a phone keypad start with 1-2-3, but a calculator with 7-8-9?

Before the push-button phone, Bell engineers tested keypad layouts: a 1960 study started with sixteen of them. The layout from adding machines, with 7-8-9 on top, was not the best.

The same grid, numbered differently, was slightly faster: 4.92 seconds against 5.08. So phones got 1-2-3 on top. Why adding machines put 7-8-9 on top? The study doesn't say.

Why do we change the clocks? It started in World War I.

Benjamin Franklin only joked about it: his 1784 essay was satire. In 1895, New Zealand insect collector George Hudson proposed moving the clocks two hours every spring, and was ridiculed. In 1907, William Willett paid for a pamphlet, "The Waste of Daylight".

Then came the war. On 30 April 1916, Germany and Austria-Hungary became the first countries to introduce daylight saving time, to save coal. Britain passed its law that May.
